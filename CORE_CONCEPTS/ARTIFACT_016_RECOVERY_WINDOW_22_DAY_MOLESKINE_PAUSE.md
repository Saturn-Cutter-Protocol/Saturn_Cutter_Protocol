# Artifact 016 — Recovery Window / 22-Day Moleskine Pause

## Date
24.08.2026

## Observation
During review of the Moleskine operational record on 23–24 August 2026, the last completed Moleskine entry was identified as 01–02 August 2026. No subsequent Saturday Moleskine processing sessions were recorded during the following approximately 22-day period.

The operator subsequently reopened the Moleskine and resumed processing without an additional compensatory workload or requirement to "catch up" through artificial activity.

## Finding
The 22-day interruption in the usual Saturday Moleskine processing cycle is consistent with a recovery window following prolonged operational load rather than evidence, by itself, of disciplinary failure or system abandonment.

The absence of Moleskine activity during this interval did not prevent the broader operating system from resuming. The return occurred naturally after the pause.

## Conclusion
Recovery Window is confirmed as a legitimate phase of the operator's current longitudinal operating cycle.

A temporary reduction or cessation of methodological documentation should not automatically be classified as dropout, loss of discipline, or failure of H-ACS. After prolonged operational load, non-production time may function as recovery capacity required for subsequent execution.

## Operational Implication
Future H-ACS cycle reviews should distinguish at minimum between:

- Recovery Window;
- Operational overload;
- Avoidance / unresolved cycles;
- Methodology abandonment;
- Deliberate discontinuation of a protocol.

The mere absence of an expected documentation artifact is insufficient evidence to classify the underlying system state.

## Evidence Status
Operational Observation — longitudinal record, single operator.

## Source
Moleskine operational record; August 2026 retrospective processing; longitudinal H-ACS records.

## Boundary
This artifact records a methodological observation about recovery and documentation continuity. It does not claim that every future period of non-documentation is restorative or productive. Each interval requires contextual evaluation.
